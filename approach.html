Approach

Why play works.

Play is how children do their most important work. A game of peekaboo or a tower that topples asks a child to watch, wait, take a turn, handle a surprise, and find a way to ask for “again.” Each of those moments exercises the attention, language, and self-control that developing bodies and brains are building, and the warmth of a trusted grown-up gives that learning a safe place to land. BrightPlay brings both together, playful practice and close connection, in four approaches. Choose one to see what it involves.

Relationships

Theraplay® Informed Practice

Theraplay® is a relationship-based approach from The Theraplay® Institute, grounded in decades of attachment research. In short, guided games, you and your child play face to face, often with nothing more than a cotton ball, a little lotion, or a sheet of newspaper to punch through.

Every session blends four dimensions of a secure relationship: structure, a calm and confident lead; engagement, attention and shared delight; nurture, comfort and care; and challenge, small stretches that build confidence. Children come away feeling seen and capable, and parents often discover a new ease in the everyday.

Ellis is completing the Theraplay® certification practicum under a Certified Theraplay® Supervisor. Sessions are video recorded for supervision; our Privacy Policy explains how we protect every recording.

Learn more: The Theraplay® Institute

Suited for

Any family that wants more ease and closeness, especially when big feelings, transitions, or new chapters (a sibling, a move, a new school) make connection harder to find.

How it unfolds

  1. A relaxed introductory conversation.
  2. A parent consultation, adults only, about your family’s story and hopes.
  3. A playful observation in the studio (the Marschak Interaction Method): you and your child try a few simple activities together.
  4. Feedback and planning: we look at the highlights together and choose a focus.
  5. Regular parent-child sessions, typically starting with about 16 weeks, with parent check-ins along the way.
  6. A shared review of how far you’ve come.

Interplay

Project ImPACT

Project ImPACT (Improving Parents As Communication Teachers) is an evidence-based, parent-mediated program developed by Brooke Ingersoll, PhD, and Anna Dvortcsak, MS, CCC-SLP. Rather than adding appointments to your week, it puts proven strategies in your hands, to use during the play and daily moments you already share.

Coaching centers on four areas that grow together: social engagement, communication, imitation, and play. A simple framework, F.A.C.T.S., gives each session its shape: Focus on your child, Adjust your communication, Create opportunities, Teach new skills, and Shape the interaction. You try each strategy with your own child, and we fine-tune it together on the spot.

Ellis completed introductory and advanced training with Anna Dvortcsak and has delivered Project ImPACT with families through a regional center provider.

Learn more: Project ImPACT

Suited for

Children up to age six whose social and communication skills are still unfolding, with or without a diagnosis.

How it unfolds

  1. A relaxed introductory conversation.
  2. A home visit with a ten-minute play sample, recorded with your permission, so you can later see how far you’ve come together.
  3. Goal setting, side by side: you choose what matters most.
  4. Coaching inside your own routines: 60-minute sessions, once or twice a week, over 12 to 24 weeks.
  5. A fresh play sample and a review together.

Expression

Functional communication

Functional communication is a reliable way to get a message across when it matters: a request, a refusal, a question, or a joke. When spoken words are few, still emerging, or hard to reach in the moment, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) opens other routes.

AAC can be as simple as handing over a picture or pointing to a picture board, or as sophisticated as a speech-generating device on a tablet. We help you find the tools that suit your child, practice them in real moments across the day, and bring teachers and caregivers along, so your child is understood wherever they go.

AAC meets the evidence-based practice criteria of the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice (2020). Our coaching supports, and never replaces, assessment or device recommendations by a licensed speech-language pathologist.

Learn more: National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice

Suited for

Children who are still finding the way of communicating that works best for them, whether they use a few words, many, or none yet.

How it unfolds

  1. A relaxed introductory conversation.
  2. Discovery, in the studio, at home, or at school: how your child already communicates, and what they most want to say.
  3. Choosing tools together, such as picture exchange, picture boards, or a speech-generating device, working alongside your child’s speech-language pathologist or school team whenever applicable.
  4. Practice across the day, including with teachers and caregivers, so the message lands everywhere.

Belonging · Coming soon

Group programs

Theraplay®-informed groups bring the warmth of Theraplay® into classrooms and community settings. Led by one or more expert guides, cooperative games help every child feel part of the circle, from preschool through elementary school.

UCLA PEERS® for Preschoolers is a 16-week, evidence-based friendship program developed at UCLA by Dr. Elizabeth Laugeson, for children ages four to six. Children practice making and keeping friends together, while parents meet in parallel sessions to support those skills at home and on playdates.

Learn more: UCLA PEERS® Clinic, The Theraplay® Institute

Suited for

Preschool, kindergarten, and elementary-age children who are ready to practice the art of friendship.

Observations, not diagnosis

Play-based observations, including the Marschak Interaction Method (MIM) where it fits, shape our coaching goals and session plans. They aren’t a medical, psychological, or developmental diagnosis, and they don’t replace a clinical evaluation. For concerns about your child’s health or development, please consult your pediatrician. We’re happy to help you find the right community resources.
