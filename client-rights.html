Client Rights

Effective date: [date of publication]

Every child and family deserves respectful, individual care. BrightPlay™ honors the rights of each child and caregiver, including the rights of people with developmental disabilities under California’s Lanterman Developmental Disabilities Services Act.

Your rights as a BrightPlay family

You and your child have the right to:

  1. Dignity, privacy, and respect. Every session follows your child’s interests and comfort.
  2. Safety. We do not use restraint or seclusion. In an emergency, we may briefly guide a child away from immediate danger. For studio and home sessions, a parent or authorized caregiver takes part and stays on site.
  3. Comfort with every activity. Some activities include gentle, caring touch, such as rubbing lotion on hands or making powder handprints, or a parent offering a small snack. You’re right there, you’re welcome to ask about any activity first, and we stop anything your child doesn’t enjoy.
  4. Make choices. You help choose goals, activities, and pace, and you may decline any activity.
  5. Clear information before you agree to services, including the purpose and limits of coaching, Ellis’s training, fees, privacy practices, and any proposed recording.
  6. Informed consent. Recording, sharing information with a supervisor, and any use of photos each require your separate written permission, which you may withdraw for the future at any time.
  7. Confidentiality and discretion, within the limits of the law, as our Privacy Policy describes.
  8. Access to your records, as our Privacy Policy describes.
  9. Raise concerns without retaliation, and end services at any time, subject to the cancellation terms in your services agreement.
  10. A prompt response to any concern about your child’s safety.

The people in your child’s life

Nannies, au pairs, grandparents, and house managers are welcome. You tell us in writing who may attend sessions, who may receive updates, and who may pick up your child, and we follow that list. The first time we meet someone new on your list, we may ask for photo ID.

When parents share decision-making, we ask for the details that apply, such as custody orders or a parenting plan, before services begin, and we follow them. BrightPlay provides coaching, not custody evaluations or expert testimony. Our services agreement explains how we respond if a subpoena or court order requires our records or time.

Non-discrimination

We do not discriminate in providing services on the basis of race, religion, age, sex, disability, or national origin of the person served or their parents, guardian, or conservator. Nor do we discriminate on the basis of color, ancestry, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, citizenship, primary language, immigration status, military or veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state, or local law.

Child safety

We follow California’s child abuse reporting laws. If we have reason to suspect a child is being harmed, we make a report to the appropriate agency. We explain this limit on confidentiality before services begin.

Communication and language

We welcome every way of communicating, including speech, gestures, signs, pictures, and communication devices. We offer services in English and Dutch. If another language would help your family, let us know and we’ll work out options together.

Concerns and complaints

  1. Start with us, if you like. Share any concern with Ellis van den Boogaard at hello@brightplay.org or (858) 224-2377.
  2. Written review. If we haven’t resolved your concern, send us a written description. We acknowledge it within five business days and send a written response within 15 business days.
  3. Outside help, at any time. You may contact a public agency or advocate whenever you choose. You never need to use our process first, and using it doesn’t limit any of your rights.

For individuals served by a regional center: you may contact your regional center service coordinator, or the Office of Clients’ Rights Advocacy (OCRA) at Disability Rights California, which provides free legal information, advice, and representation to regional center clients: 1-800-390-7032 (TTY 877-669-6023).

What we provide, and what we don’t

BrightPlay provides educational, play-based parent-child coaching. We do not provide medical or psychological diagnosis, psychotherapy, applied behavior analysis (ABA), speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, custody evaluations, or emergency services. Our observations inform coaching goals and are not a diagnosis.

In an emergency, call 911. For a mental health crisis, call or text 988.
